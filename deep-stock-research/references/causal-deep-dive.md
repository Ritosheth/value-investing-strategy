# Causal deep-dive standard

Use this standard for the 3–5 issues most capable of changing the investment conclusion. Depth means resolving the investment implication, not accumulating background facts.

## Required causal chain

For each issue, answer in order:

1. **Fact/change:** What happened, compared with what baseline, as of which date?
2. **Exposure:** Which product, geography, customer, supplier, asset, cost, or financing channel is affected? Quantify its share or bound it when possible.
3. **Mechanism:** Why should the fact change volume, price, mix, yield, utilization, cost, tax, working capital, capex, funding, or risk premium?
4. **Financial transmission:** Which line items and valuation inputs move, in which direction, over what horizon? Show a sensitivity when defensible.
5. **Second-order effects:** How might competitors, customers, suppliers, regulators, currencies, or capital allocation respond?
6. **Scenarios:** State bear/base/bull paths, their necessary conditions, and an evidence-based directional judgment. Do not invent numeric probabilities.
7. **Disconfirmation:** Identify the strongest alternative explanation and evidence that would falsify the preferred path.
8. **Monitoring:** Give dated or threshold-based leading indicators, primary sources, and the next decision point.

Use the compact form `事实 → 暴露 → 机制 → 财务影响 → 估值/姿态 → 验证信号` in the report. Clearly label inference. If exposure or mechanism cannot be established, say the issue is not yet decision-useful and reduce confidence; do not pad it with generic commentary.

## Materiality and stopping rule

Prioritize an issue when a reasonable scenario could change the posture, earnings path, cash needs, valuation multiple, or primary invalidation trigger. Spend less space on background that cannot reach those outputs.

A deep dive is complete only when it has:

- at least one primary-source fact and its date;
- a company-specific exposure, quantified or explicitly unavailable;
- a causal mechanism and financial direction;
- a competing path or contradiction;
- a forward indicator that can confirm or falsify the conclusion.

“关系友好,” “行业景气,” “政策支持,” “竞争加剧,” “产能释放,” and “需要关注” are starting hypotheses, never terminal conclusions.

## Overseas factory and cross-border expansion

Assess each material overseas site as a small investment case rather than one risk bullet:

- **Strategic role:** customer proximity, tariff or supply-chain diversification, capacity, product mix, and whether the site is additive or replaces domestic output.
- **Execution:** construction and ramp schedule, certification, yield, utilization, labor/management localization, supplier localization, utilities, logistics, capex, depreciation, and working capital.
- **Unit economics:** incentives, tax holiday expiry, wage/power/logistics costs, FX, transfer pricing, profit repatriation, and incremental margin/ROIC. Separate one-quarter breakeven from sustainable cash profitability.
- **Policy and geopolitics:** host-country political continuity, bilateral relations, investment treaties, local industrial policy, permits, ownership rules, sanctions/export controls, tariffs, anti-circumvention, and rules of origin. Include the destination market and customer jurisdiction, not only China–host relations.
- **Competitive response:** peers' local capacity, customer dual-sourcing, local ecosystem formation, and whether geographic diversification becomes an industry baseline rather than a moat.

Do not infer commercial safety from diplomatic friendliness alone. Ask which policy is binding, whether it is implemented, how long it lasts, which revenue/cost line it changes, and what could reverse it.

Example framing for a Thailand PCB plant:

`中泰关系稳定/投资合作深化` may lower tail-risk perception and support permits or coordination, but it does not by itself prove US/EU market access, origin eligibility, customer certification, stable incentives, or profitable ramp. Test Thai BOI/EEC policy, tax and ownership terms, rules of origin and destination-market trade rules, utilities/labor/logistics, customer approvals, site utilization/yield, and cash profitability. Then state whether the plant is most likely to be a near-term margin drag, a medium-term resilience asset, or a durable return enhancer—and what evidence would change that judgment.

## Source discipline for causal claims

- Match the source to the claim: company filings for exposure and economics; statutes/regulators/customs for binding rules; official bilateral documents for diplomatic commitments; industry or customer evidence for demand and certification.
- Triangulate thesis-critical cross-border claims across at least two independent primary-source perspectives when available.
- Separate announcement date, effective date, implementation status, and company realization date.
- Record unresolved links as data gaps and state how they affect the directional conclusion.
